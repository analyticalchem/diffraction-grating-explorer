About the Model and the Figure

Grating equation

mλ = d(sin θ + sin φ), with groove spacing d = 1/N. Both angles are measured from the grating normal, and φ is positive on the same side as the incident beam, so the 0th order (the mirror reflection) leaves at φ = −θ. An order exists only when |mλ/d − sin θ| ≤ 1.

Figure

  • Styled after the chart “Light Diffraction from Grating” in Diffraction Grating Light Angles.xlsm: white incident beam and 0th order, thick, medium and thin lines for the 1st, 2nd and 3rd orders, and dashed lines for negative orders.
  • Angles are drawn to true scale. The Excel chart’s axes stretch it sideways, which makes small angles look larger.
  • Beam colors are approximate screen colors for each wavelength. Ultraviolet and infrared lines are drawn gray.

White light

Each order is drawn as a continuous 380–780 nm spectrum that dims toward both ends. Where orders overlap their colors add: 1st-order 760–780 nm leaves at the same angles as 2nd-order 380–390 nm, because mλ is the same.

Exit slit

  • An ideal, zero-width slit at angle φ receives λ = d(sin θ + sin φ)/m from every order at once.
  • A slit of width w at distance L, perpendicular to the diffracted beam, spans Δφ = 2·tan⁻¹(w/2L). Each order then passes a band of width Δλ = d[sin(φ + Δφ/2) − sin(φ − Δφ/2)]/m ≈ d·cos φ·Δφ/m.
  • Resolution R = λ/Δλ = (sin θ + sin φ)/[sin(φ + Δφ/2) − sin(φ − Δφ/2)]. Because λ and Δλ both scale as 1/m, R at a fixed slit angle is the same in every order. A narrower slit, a longer distance to the slit, or a larger diffraction angle raises R.
  • The incident beam is treated as a collimated laser, so no entrance-slit image adds to the bandpass.

Monochromator mode

  • The entrance and exit slits stay fixed, an included angle 2K apart, and the grating turns on its mount by ψ from the instrument axis that bisects them. Then θ = ψ + K and φ = ψ − K.
  • Switching the mode on keeps the incident angle, the slit angle and the grating’s orientation you already have: 2K = θ − φ and ψ = (θ + φ)/2, and the grating stays flat. The Grating rotation slider then turns it up to 45° either way from there, adding the same angle to θ and φ, as long as both beams stay in front of the grating. Turning past the incident beam makes θ negative, with the beam arriving from the right of the normal. 2K is negative when the exit slit sits on the incident side, beyond the entrance; the layout is then the mirror image of the usual one.
  • Substituting into the grating equation gives mλ = 2d·cos K·sin ψ, the sine drive that tunes a scanning monochromator. The wavelength reaching the exit slit is proportional to sin ψ.
  • At ψ = 0 the 0th order, the mirror reflection, goes out the exit slit. K = 0 is the Littrow mount, where the diffracted light returns along the incident path.
  • In the figure the slits stay put and the grating, its normal and its angle scale turn together. The dashed instrument axis and the ψ arc show the angle the sine drive uses. The Diffraction Angles table still lists angles from the grating normal.
